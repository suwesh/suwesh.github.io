There are engineering problems you discover because they are difficult.

And then there are the ones you discover when you are really, really lazy.

For me, it was QA.

I was temporarily assigned GUI testing work to learn a large enterprise application.

The assignment made sense. The business application had complicated workflows, hidden dependencies, and enough business rules to punish anyone who clicked through it without understanding the process and following the correct flow, and skipping any step would result in a painful list of validations later on that would make anyone reconsider their employment.

There was only one problem.

I did not want to keep doing these testing, especially not manually.

## The Job

Each testing request followed roughly the same routine.

Someone (either developer or product owner) would explain what the new change was and what needed to be tested. But to test these changes one had to reconstruct the existing business processes and arrive at the stage where the new change was introduced, then perform the testing considering multiple scenarios, capture screenshots and make a .docx evidence file. This was the only part of my job I hated. The clicking were repetitive, but the thinking around them were not.

Fortunately, I had already built a RAG chatbot (the one I built in Story 000) using the applications user manuals, FAQs and training video transcripts. Whenever I did not know how to complete a business flow, I asked the chatbot, and it told me what to do and how to do it.

But I still had to perform the manual labor. And again, I hated how much of my time and efforts disappeared into something that was pretty meaningless to me and my future.

## My Completely Reasonable Response

I decided to build my replacement, a machine that would do this job for me.

The first version of the idea was simple:<br>
Given a Vision Language Model capable of Computer-Use the current screenshot -> Tell it what needs to be achieved -> Let it decide the next action -> Orchestrate the action -> Capture the new screen -> Repeat.

Although the <a href="https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-4/">Open AI GPT 5.4 with its advertised SOTA Computer Use and Vision</a> capabilities was really attractive to me, I had to find an open-weight alternative as I needed a working prototype and PoC first to show the management, only then I would get approval for GPT 5.4 API subscription. So I set out on my journey to find the best VLM with CUA capability for my use case while fitting on a 12 GB VRAM card.

That led me to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. I downloaded the model, loaded it through LM Studio, and began with a very basic isolated validation. I gave the model screenshots of the application and asked questions such as:<br>
- To complete this goal, what is the next atomic action needed?
- Has the previous action changed the interface as expected?

And the answers were good enough to prove that a tighter system instruction would make the outputs more precise and robust.
